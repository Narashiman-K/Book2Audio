# The Lantern Keeper

*A short book about finding light you can carry.*

## Chapter One: The House at the End of the Pier

Mara had never believed the stories about the lantern keeper. Stories, her grandmother always said, were the shadows that people cast when they were afraid of the truth. But on the night the storm took the ferry, Mara walked the length of the old pier anyway, and the wooden boards sang under her feet like they remembered her name.

The house at the end of the pier had one window, and the window had one light, and the light did not flicker the way flames flicker. It breathed. Mara stood in the rain for a long time before she found the courage to knock.

"You're late," said a voice from inside. "But never too late. That is the difference between the lost and the found."

## Chapter Two: What the Light Remembers

The keeper was older than the sea and younger than the morning. He showed her the lantern: a small thing of brass and glass, warm as bread from an oven, and utterly ordinary until you looked away.

"Every lantern remembers the people who carried it," he said. "A fisherman who wanted one more season. A girl who was walking home in the dark. A man who never learned to read but knew every star by heart."

Mara touched the glass and felt something move inside her chest — not warmth exactly, but the promise of warmth, like a letter you have written but not sent.

"This is your dream now," the keeper said, and he did not smile when he said it, because dreams are not a smiling matter. Dreams are work.

## Chapter Three: The Weight of a Small Flame

Carrying light is harder than carrying darkness. Anyone can be tired; only the hopeful can be tired and still begin again.

On the third night, the wind came back and took the pier's last railing. On the fourth night, the rain found the one seam in the roof and made a small sea on the floor. And every night, the lantern asked the same question in its silent way: will you hold me tomorrow too?

"Yes," Mara said. "Always."

## Chapter Four: The Passage

There is a passage in every keeper's life where the light must be carried further than the keeper has ever walked. For Mara it came on the ninth night, when a stranger's boat lost its lantern in the swell, and the only light left in the world that could reach them was hers.

She wrapped the lantern in her coat, and the coat in her arms, and she walked into weather that had no manners and no mercy.

She walked because the light was not a thing she owned. It was a thing she owed.

## Chapter Five: What You Keep

Years later, children from the town would come to the pier and ask the woman with the bright window whether the stories were true.

"The stories are boats," Mara would tell them. "They are built to be sailed, not admired."

"Is that a story?" the children would ask.

"That," she said, handing them the lantern, "is the truth: that what you keep by holding it is the only thing worth keeping."

## Chapter Six: The Keeper's Answer

The keeper never explained where the lantern came from, and in the end Mara understood why. Some truths must be carried, not told.

But she left a note in the drawer of the little brass door, where all keepers leave their notes, and the note said only this:

*To whoever holds this next — it is heavier than it looks, and lighter than you fear. Begin.*
